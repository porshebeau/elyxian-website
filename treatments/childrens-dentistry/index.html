Who it suits

Is it right for you?

We suggest a first visit by your child’s first birthday, or when the first tooth appears. Come sooner if you notice white or brown spots on the teeth, if a fall has knocked their teeth, or if you have any concern.

Baby teeth matter. They help children eat and speak, and hold space for the adult teeth. Decay in baby teeth can cause pain and infection, and can affect the adult teeth underneath.

What we can help with

  • Check-ups and gentle cleaning
  • Fluoride varnish to strengthen enamel
  • Fissure sealants on back teeth
  • Fillings for baby and adult teeth
  • Advice on teething, thumb-sucking and dummies
  • Knocked or broken teeth after a fall

What to expect

What happens, step by step

  1. 1

    Getting comfortable

    Your child can sit on your lap for the first look. We explain what we will do in words they understand, and show them the instruments.

  2. 2

    A gentle check

    We count and check the teeth, look at the gums and bite, and see how the adult teeth are coming through.

  3. 3

    Prevention

    Depending on what we find, we may suggest fluoride varnish or sealants to help protect the teeth from decay.

  4. 4

    Advice for home

    We show you how to brush your child’s teeth, and talk about food, drinks and bedtime bottles.

Aftercare

What you can do at home

Brush twice a day with fluoride toothpaste: a smear the size of a grain of rice under three, and a pea-sized amount from three to six. Help with brushing until around age seven or eight.

Keep sugary snacks and drinks to mealtimes, and give only water between meals and at night. Avoid putting a child to bed with a bottle of milk or juice.

If an adult tooth is knocked out, put it back in the socket if you can, or keep it in milk, and see a dentist straight away. Do not put a baby tooth back.

Questions

What people ask about children’s dentistry

When should my child first see a dentist?

By their first birthday, or when the first tooth appears. An early visit is mostly about getting familiar with the clinic and getting advice, before any problem appears.

Do cavities in baby teeth need treating if they will fall out anyway?

Often, yes. Some baby teeth stay until age 11 or 12, and decay can cause pain, infection and early loss of the tooth, which can affect how the adult teeth come in. We advise on each tooth.

My child is scared of the dentist. What can I do?

Talk about the visit calmly and simply, without words like ‘hurt’ or ‘injection’, and never use the dentist as a threat. Tell us when you book, and we will go at your child’s pace.

Can I stay with my child during the visit?

Yes. You are welcome in the room throughout.
