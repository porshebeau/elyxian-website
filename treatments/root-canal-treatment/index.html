Root canal treatment (RCT) in Lajpat Nagar

Commonly known as RCT, root canal treatment cares for inflamed or infected tissue inside a tooth so the natural tooth can be preserved. It is done under local anaesthesia, and for most people feels much like having a routine filling.

Who it suits

Is it right for you?

Inside each tooth is soft tissue, called the pulp, that holds nerves and blood vessels. Deep decay, a crack or an injury can let bacteria reach it, and it becomes inflamed or infected. It cannot heal by itself.

Sometimes there are no symptoms at all, and the problem shows up on an X-ray. Not every painful tooth needs a root canal, so we find the cause first.

If you have facial swelling that is spreading, a fever, or difficulty swallowing or breathing, seek emergency care at once. See the emergency instructions.

Signs you may need one

  • Tooth pain that lingers, or wakes you at night
  • Sensitivity to hot or cold that stays after the hot or cold is gone
  • Pain when you bite or press on the tooth
  • Swelling, or a small pimple on the gum near a tooth
  • A tooth that has darkened

What to expect

What happens, step by step

  1. 1

    Diagnosis

    We examine the tooth, take an X-ray, and may test how it responds to cold. We explain whether it can be saved, and what the alternative would be.

  2. 2

    Cleaning the canals

    Under local anaesthesia, with a thin rubber sheet (a dental dam) keeping the tooth dry, we remove the inflamed or infected pulp and clean and shape the canals inside the root.

  3. 3

    Sealing

    The canals are filled and sealed. This takes one visit or more, depending on the tooth and any infection. A temporary filling protects the tooth between visits.

  4. 4

    Protecting the tooth

    A treated tooth, especially a back tooth, can be more brittle, so many need a crown to stop them breaking.

Aftercare

After treatment

The tooth may be tender for a few days, particularly when you bite. Ordinary pain relief usually helps. Contact us if pain or swelling gets worse rather than better.

Avoid chewing hard food on that side until the tooth has its final filling or crown.

Then look after it as you would any tooth: brushing, cleaning between your teeth, and regular check-ups.

Questions

What people ask about root canal treatment

Is root canal treatment painful?

It is done under local anaesthesia, so it should feel much like a filling. The pain people associate with root canals usually comes from the infection beforehand, which the treatment relieves.

How many visits will I need?

Some teeth are treated in one visit. Others need two or more, particularly back teeth with several canals or teeth with an active infection. We tell you at the start.

Do I need a crown afterwards?

Often, especially for back teeth, which take the most force when you chew. A front tooth with little damage may only need a filling. We advise you based on how much tooth remains.

Isn’t it simpler to take the tooth out?

Extraction is an option, but a gap can let neighbouring teeth drift and make chewing harder, and replacing the tooth with an implant or bridge is usually more involved. When a tooth can be saved, keeping it is generally the better choice.
