Who it suits

Is it right for you?

A check-up suits anyone who hasn’t seen a dentist in a while, and anyone who has noticed a change: a chipped or rough tooth, gums that bleed when you brush, a new sensitivity to hot or cold, or a filling that feels different.

Decay and gum disease often cause no pain in their early stages. A regular examination finds them while treatment is still small, which usually means a filling rather than a root canal, or a clean rather than more involved gum treatment.

A visit can include

  • An examination of your teeth, gums, tongue and the soft tissues of your mouth
  • X-rays, only when they are needed
  • Scaling and polishing to remove hardened deposits
  • Fillings for small cavities
  • Advice on brushing and cleaning between your teeth

What to expect

What happens, step by step

  1. 1

    Your history

    We ask about any concerns, your medical history and the medicines you take, since some of them affect your teeth and gums.

  2. 2

    The examination

    We check each tooth, your gums, your bite and the soft tissues of your mouth, including a look for any unusual patches. X-rays are taken only if they are needed to answer a specific question.

  3. 3

    Cleaning

    If you need it, a professional clean (scaling and polishing) removes hardened deposits that a toothbrush can’t. It can feel scratchy, but it shouldn’t hurt. Tell us if it does.

  4. 4

    Your plan

    We explain what we found, show you on the X-ray or a photograph where that helps, and agree any next steps and their cost before anything more is done.

Aftercare

Between visits

Brush twice a day with fluoride toothpaste, clean between your teeth once a day, and keep sugary food and drinks to mealtimes.

Gums can feel slightly tender after a deep clean, and teeth may be briefly sensitive. This usually settles within a few days.

We suggest when to come back based on your own teeth and gums. For many people that is every six to twelve months.

Questions

What people ask about dental check-ups and cleaning

How often do I need a check-up?

It depends on your teeth, your gums and your general health. Many people are seen every six to twelve months, and some more often. We will suggest an interval that suits you.

Does scaling damage the enamel?

No. Professional cleaning removes hardened deposits (tartar) from the surface of the tooth without removing enamel. Teeth can feel different or slightly sensitive afterwards, because surfaces that were covered are exposed again. This usually settles within days.

Do I need X-rays at every visit?

No. X-rays are taken when they are needed to answer a specific question, such as checking between teeth or under a filling. How often depends on your risk of decay and gum disease.

My gums bleed when I brush. Should I stop?

No. Bleeding is often an early sign of gum inflammation caused by plaque, and gentle, thorough cleaning usually helps. If it carries on for more than a week or two, book a check-up.
